Every Monday at 17:00 UTC, a scheduled job scans the open web for fintech market signals. Not what banks say about innovation. What fintech players actually do — launches, funding, partnerships, and infrastructure moves.

The radar watches eight search areas: neobank and digital bank launches, fintech funding and M&A, API economy and open banking, embedded finance and BNPL, crypto and stablecoin banking, AI-fintech patents and IP, RegTech and SupTech, and cross-border real-time payments. Each finding gets mapped to BIAN service domains, assessed through six innovation lenses, and ranked by signal strength — new, strengthening, or fading.

The lenses are the point. Six questions that turn fintech signals into strategy:

- **Commoditization pressure**: which banking products are being commoditized by fintech, and where does the bank still add value?
- **Distribution disruption**: how are fintech players changing how banking products reach customers?
- **Build vs partner vs buy**: should the bank build, partner, or acquire the capability a fintech demonstrates?
- **Customer acquisition cost**: how do fintech CAC compare to bank channels, and where is the inflection point?
- **Platform play**: which fintech capabilities could become platform infrastructure the bank builds on?
- **Regulatory arbitrage**: where are fintech players exploiting regulatory gaps the bank can or should close?

The radar runs because banking products are becoming commodities. The scan catches the signals that tell you which infrastructure to build, which capabilities to partner for, and which markets to defend.
