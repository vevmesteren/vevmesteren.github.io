A stablecoin company now holds a US national trust bank charter. In the same week, BNY and Robinhood tied themselves closer to it.

On October 26, Ripple announced final OCC approval for Ripple National Trust Bank. Once operating, it will manage the reserves behind RLUSD, act as collateral trustee and offer fiduciary custody of digital assets. It can't take deposits or lend. It doesn't need to.

BNY, already RLUSD's primary reserve custodian, plans to let clients mint and redeem RLUSD directly. Robinhood is making RLUSD available across its platform. Ripple says it will exit 2026 at a $900 million annualized product revenue run rate.

Ripple Treasury also now lets corporate customers put idle operating cash to work outside banking hours, through RLUSD and tokenized money market funds. Settlement and yield running around the clock, from a non-bank, under federal supervision. Banks still move much of this in batch windows.

Meanwhile the privacy layer is arriving. On November 9, Raycash, a Zama spin-out, launched in private beta on Ethereum mainnet with 75,000+ people on its waitlist. It uses fully homomorphic encryption, so balances and amounts stay encrypted on-chain while people hold dollars, pay each other and earn yield through Morpho.

FHE lets you compute on data without decrypting it. For agent payments that matters. An agent could transact on a public chain without publishing its owner's balance and history. It is one candidate for that job, next to zero-knowledge proofs and secure enclaves, and it is now in a shipping product.

I see trust splitting into three layers:

- Regulatory: charters and accreditation that decide who is allowed to play.
- Institutional: the balance sheet, liability and name standing behind a transaction.
- Cryptographic: guarantees that hold without trusting any single party.

Moving money is getting cheaper and more alike everywhere. My bet is that the value moves to whoever can supply all three layers of trust together.
