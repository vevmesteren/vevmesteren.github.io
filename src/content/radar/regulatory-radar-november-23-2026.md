Canada moved toward operational open banking on November 20, when Finance Minister Champagne designated Financial Data Exchange (FDX) as the technical standards body and the Ombudsman for Banking Services and Investments (OBSI) as the external complaints body for the Consumer-Driven Banking Framework. The ministerial orders appeared in Canada Gazette Part I on November 21. FDX will maintain a sovereign Canadian standard for secure, API-based data sharing, replacing the screen-scraping practices that approximately nine million Canadians currently rely on.

OSFI amended its third-party risk management guideline, B-10, on November 19 to align with the consumer-driven banking framework. Institutions should rely on federal accreditation of participating entities rather than duplicating risk assessments. They remain responsible for residual cyber and data integrity risks. The amendment took effect immediately.

Payments Canada tightened its compliance regime the same day. Amendments to its Compliance By-law, published in Canada Gazette Part II on November 18, raise the maximum penalty per contravention from $250,000 to $1,000,000 and introduce an expedited investigation process. The changes apply to Lynx, the Automated Clearing Settlement System, and the Real-Time Rail, which launched November 9 with mandatory centralized fraud services.

Internationally, the Hong Kong Monetary Authority published the most detailed agentic AI governance proposal from any prudential regulator to date. The November 2 Discussion Paper proposes intent-anchored accountability, a registry of material AI agents, a kill switch, and Know Your Agent requirements. Responses are due February 1, 2027. A BIS speech on November 3 named agentic financial transactions as a financial stability concern for the first time, and the EBA revised its internal governance guidelines on November 5 to bring AI and digitalisation into scope under CRD VI and DORA.
