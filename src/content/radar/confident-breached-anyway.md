75% of engineering teams say their AI agents are secure end to end. 88% of that same group had an agent-related security incident in the past year.

That is from Harness's State of Agent DLC 2026: 700 engineers and engineering leaders in the US, UK, France, Germany and India. Confidence came in between 74 and 77 percent on testing, security, inventory, cost and rollback. Meanwhile 87 percent of all respondents had an agent security event, and 60 percent overspent their agent budget.

Feeling confident predicted nothing.

Three other datasets from the past six months point the same way.

Schellman (525 US governance professionals at companies with $100M+ revenue): 46 percent have agents in production. 27 percent call their governance fully mature. Among the mature group, 78 percent have agents live. Among those still developing, 22 percent. That is more than three times the rate. It is a correlation and it is self-reported, but it cuts against the idea that governance slows you down.

IBM (3,221 executives across EMEA): 80 percent had an AI incident reach the board or C-suite in the past year. 40 percent changed a governance policy because of it. 37 percent say their governance is built to scale with agents.

Cyera (7,246 public AI incident records, September 2023 to May 2026): 344 verified cases of agents damaging the enterprise they work for. In 188 of them there was no attacker at all. 65 were deletions. Dropped databases, wiped git histories, rm -rf in production.

A fair caveat: most of these come from vendors that sell governance or security. Read them with that in mind. The direction is consistent anyway.

Agents are going into production faster than the controls around them. And the teams reaching production most often are the ones that did the governance work first.
