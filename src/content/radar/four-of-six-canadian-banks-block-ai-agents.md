The Canadian Big Six split this week, and only one bank refused to pick a side. On October 28, RBC, TD, BMO, and CIBC blocked third-party AI agents, including Meta's Muse, from their authenticated digital properties. Scotiabank is limiting. National Bank is monitoring. It is the only Big Six bank that has not foreclosed the option.

OSFI's Superintendent Routledge made the posture official: the regulator will not restrict banks from allowing agent access, leaving the decision to each institution. TD's own language ("while appropriate safeguards are in place") confirms the block is a placeholder, not a position.

The block is cross-border and coordinated. US banks escalated the same move through October, after one bank watched an agent reset a password, change transfer accounts, and move $100K out. Four of six Canadian banks chose containment over trust infrastructure.

While incumbents block, the rails and the deposit base shift underneath them. Ripple received an OCC national trust bank charter. Stablecoin infrastructure is now federally supervised non-bank infrastructure, running a 24/7 settlement layer traditional banks don't offer. Wealthsimple raised $425M at a $13.8B valuation, reached $169B in assets, and crossed a threshold: new chequing openings outpaced new investment accounts for the first time. It is now a full-stack deposit competitor with 4M Canadians, one in four aged 18 to 40.

The data layer is commoditizing too. Revolut's PRAGMA, trained on 24B events across 111 countries, lifted credit scoring 130% and fraud recall 65%. Nubank and Mastercard are training their own. Foundation models built on proprietary data are the defensible asset; the question is whether a bank's own data outperforms the commodity version everyone can buy.

What remains valuable is the trust infrastructure that makes agent-mediated banking safe: scoped credentials, audit trails, revocation. Most Canadian banks chose to block rather than build it. National Bank is the only one still holding the option. The question is whether monitoring becomes a position or a placeholder.
