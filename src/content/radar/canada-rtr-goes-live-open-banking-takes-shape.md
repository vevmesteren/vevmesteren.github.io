Canada's Real-Time Rail (RTR) went live on November 9, 2026, bringing 24/7 instant settlement and ISO 20022 data-rich messaging to the national payment system. Payments Canada launched with mandatory centralized fraud services, making Canada the first G7 country to deploy a real-time payment rail with fraud controls built in at launch. The initial cohort was fintech-heavy: Wealthsimple, KOHO, Peoples Trust, and Bank of America went live first. The Big Six banks are onboarding over the coming months.

Eleven days later, on November 20, the Department of Finance Canada designated Financial Data Exchange (FDX) as the technical standards body for the consumer-driven banking framework, and the Ombudsman for Banking Services and Investments (OBSI) as the external complaints body. The framework starts with "read" access for data sharing and will progress to "write" access for payment initiation. About nine million Canadians currently share financial data through screen scraping, which the framework will replace with API-based sharing.

The two tracks are designed to interlock. Payments Canada has positioned the RTR's real-time settlement as the backbone for the eventual payment-initiation phase of consumer-driven banking. The gap between the RTR's launch and full Big Six participation is the window where banks and fintechs build the products that sit on top of these new rails.

Globally, the build-versus-partner question for stablecoin corporate payments is resolving toward partnership. PNC Bank launched a USDC payment acceptance pilot for corporate clients through Coinbase on November 9, converting incoming stablecoins to dollars automatically. Citi expanded a similar program in September. Deutsche Bank went live with institutional crypto custody under MiCA on November 11.

The OCC's final stablecoin rules under the GENIUS Act take effect January 18, 2027, creating regulated pathways for banks as issuers, custodians, and reserve holders. Separately, AI agents are reaching payment infrastructure. Pliant raised $80 million on November 4 to open its B2B card rails to AI agents, with explicit expansion into Canada. Payhawk raised $280 million on November 18 to build a platform pairing AI agents with payment infrastructure and policy controls.
