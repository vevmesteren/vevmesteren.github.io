Two regulatory deadlines now drive the calendar for Canadian banks deploying agentic AI. OSFI's revised Guideline E-23 takes effect May 1, 2027 — 166 days out — and for the first time folds AI and machine-learning models into the enterprise-wide model risk management framework. Institutions that have not mapped their AI/ML model inventory to E-23's lifecycle expectations by Q1 2027 will face supervisory scrutiny.

Meanwhile, Canada's Real-Time Rail launched November 9, 2026 — the country's first 24/7/365 instant payment system and the last G7 nation to go live. Canada is the first G7 country to deploy mandatory centralized fraud services at launch: confirmation of payee, fraud transaction safety scores, a fraud risk list, and a fraud reporting platform. Eight institutions joined the initial phase; major banks are expected to connect by mid-2027. The fraud services are system rules, not guidance.

Three international regulators converged on agentic AI governance within weeks of each other. The OCC issued Bulletin 2026-54 on November 4 — a supervisory framework for examiners evaluating banks' use of "Super Intelligence," structured around six pillars (Purpose, Risk, Control, Oversight, Resilience, Assurance) and risk classification based on consequence and autonomy, not technical sophistication. The HKMA published a Discussion Paper on Agentic AI Governance on November 2, proposing intent-anchored accountability, a registry of material AI agents, and kill-switch mechanisms. OSFI endorsed the FSB's 12 Sound Practices for Responsible AI Adoption on October 15.

The convergence is striking: board accountability, risk-based classification by consequence, human oversight calibrated to materiality, and third-party dependency management appear in every framework. The HKMA's "intent-anchored accountability" externally validates the intent-layer framing in BNC's agentic AI strategy — but the registry, kill-switch, and agent harness control concepts are operational requirements the strategy does not yet address.

FATF launched a targeted review of virtual asset standards on October 30, responding to uneven global implementation of Recommendation 15. Twenty-two percent of assessed jurisdictions remain non-compliant. Initial results are due June and October 2027. FINTRAC's beneficial ownership discrepancy reporting obligations — in force since October 2025 — require ongoing integration with Corporations Canada's ISC database for high-risk CBCA corporations.
