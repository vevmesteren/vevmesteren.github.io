Singapore's central bank proposed the first regulatory identity standard for AI agents in banking. MAS Managing Director Chia Der Jiun announced the "Know Your Agent" protocol at the Singapore FinTech Festival: institutions verify an agent's identity, authority, and mandate before allowing transactions, then open a dedicated secure channel for agent access. The protocol covers who the agent is, whom it represents, what authority it holds, and what assurance exists about its provenance and reliability.

MAS paired the protocol with an Agentic Money Sandbox, built with the Future of Finance Institute, testing AI agents against programmable money in corporate treasury and payments. The same week, MAS issued S$100 million in tokenised MAS Bills, settled natively on its SGD Test Network in wholesale central bank digital currency. Five primary dealers participated: DBS, HSBC, OCBC, Standard Chartered, and UOB.

Canada moved in parallel. The Bank of Canada designated the Real-Time Rail as a prominent payment system under the Payment Clearing and Settlement Act, effective November 8. OSFI issued a targeted amendment to Guideline B-10, extending third-party risk management to consumer-driven banking.

The Bank of England's fourth AI survey, published October 26, finds 80 percent of firms deploy AI agents for decision support. Only 2 percent grant broad autonomy. The gap between "agent recommends, human decides" and "agent acts" is where the opportunity and the risk concentrate.

The architecture for non-human actors in the financial system is being built by regulators, not vendors. The trust layer is becoming a supervisory artifact.
