The warning from the previous radar landed. On November 5, Wealthsimple launched Penny, an AI assistant that moves money, places trades, pays invoices, and flags duplicate charges, with every action client-confirmed. Alongside it: CADD, a CAD-backed stablecoin paying 3.25% yield; temporary virtual cards built for "safer agentic payments"; $100K instant transfers; and a roadmap to connect ChatGPT and Claude in early 2027.

Wealthsimple is now building the agent integration layer the Big Six chose not to. It has the scoped-authorization, audit-trail, revocable-access pattern, built by a neobank with $169B in assets and 4M Canadians, not by a chartered incumbent.

BMO answered the same week with the opposite move: AI Agent Assist for credit card service, internal only, human-in-the-loop, targeting a 20 to 30% time reduction across 500K calls a year. BMO blocked external agents on October 28 and built internal agents on November 6. The strategy is now visible across the Big Six: block external, build internal, position as the "responsible AI" bank. It is a coherent defense. It does not answer the question of what remains valuable when agents are the interface.

The regulator is building the path to sanctioned access. On November 2, the Bank of Canada published draft accreditation requirements for Consumer-Driven Banking participants, with a feedback deadline of December 8. The Competition Bureau is advocating write access and Real-Time Rail integration. The CDB Act is in force; regulations are expected in 2027, the same timeline as Wealthsimple's ChatGPT and Claude integration.

The trust stack is differentiating across three layers: regulatory (the Bank of Canada), institutional (charters and cores), and cryptographic. What remains strategically valuable is the platform that mediates between agent and financial system, the trust infrastructure that makes it safe, and the deterministic core agents act on top of. National Bank has the charter, the core, and the monitoring posture. The window narrows: build the gateway before the 2027 regulations take effect, or watch Wealthsimple become the Canadian agent platform.
