The legislative phase of Canadian open banking is over. The Consumer-Driven Banking Regulations, published in Canada Gazette Part I on June 27, operationalize the CDB Act that received royal assent in March. Phase one is read-only data sharing by API with screen scraping prohibited, the Bank of Canada as supervisor, and a twelve-month consent reconfirmation cadence. Large banks are mandated; others opt in. Derived data is carved out: proprietary analytics are not subject to mandatory sharing, which protects a bank's intelligence layer even as the customer data layer opens. Write access and payment initiation trail under a twelve-to-eighteen-month policy workstream.

McKinsey put a framework on the disintermediation question everyone has been circling. Three postures: Wait, Adapt, or Compete. The trust data is the headline: sixty-two percent of consumers trust their primary bank for generative AI financial services versus nineteen percent for tech companies. That is the moat, and it is a wasting asset if banks do not build agent capability. The lowest-cost move McKinsey identifies is Generative Engine Optimization: one bank saw six-times organic traffic growth by making product content legible to LLMs. EY, arriving at the same moment, frames the "trust-first bank" (identity, permissions, and fraud treated as the core product) as the North Star.

The settlement layer moved from concept to commercial. Fiserv's stablecoin platform went live on Solana on October 1, with Canada's VersaBank as issuer and custodian and Fireblocks handling tokenization. It is the first production, non-pilot bank stablecoin on a public chain, replacing wire transfers for over ninety community banks. Chime's $590 million acquisition of Stride Bank closed the same window, confirming that charter consolidation, not product novelty, is where scale accrues.

Why it matters: open banking regulation, agent disintermediation strategy, and stablecoin settlement all crossed from theory to implementation in the same week. The banks building API infrastructure, consent management, and AI governance now will deploy capability when the framework completes. The banks waiting will be implementing compliance, not capability.
