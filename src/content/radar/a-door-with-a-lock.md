Eight days after Canada's biggest banks started blocking outside AI agents, Wealthsimple announced how it plans to let them in. With a lock on the door.

On November 5, Wealthsimple announced 12 products. The headliner is Penny, an AI assistant that can send e-Transfers, pay invoices, flag duplicate charges and place trades. Nothing moves without the client confirming it, and it runs inside Wealthsimple's own environment.

The part I'd watch comes after Penny. In early 2027, clients will be able to connect their accounts to ChatGPT or Claude and decide how much access each one gets. Setup needs client approval, and critical actions get an extra check.

The supporting pieces:

- Temporary virtual cards, up to five at a time, each with its own number, expiry and spending limit. Wealthsimple ties them directly to "safer agentic payments."
- CADD, a Canadian-dollar stablecoin with a 3.25 percent annual reward, pending final regulatory approval.
- Instant transfers of up to $100,000 between clients, live now.

One caution: most of this is announced, not shipped. Penny and the outside connections arrive over the coming months.

Read the list as a design, though. Access the client scopes. Approval before money moves. Spending limits enforced by the card itself. That is a controlled door for agents, and it is the piece the banks that blocked haven't shown yet.

The scale matters. $169 billion in assets under administration at September 30, up from $100 billion a year earlier. More than 4 million clients. In Q2, new chequing accounts outpaced new investment accounts for the first time. Wealthsimple is now competing for the everyday deposit relationship, which is the core of a retail bank.

Blocking and building are both defensible today. Only one of them will be learning how customers actually want to delegate.
