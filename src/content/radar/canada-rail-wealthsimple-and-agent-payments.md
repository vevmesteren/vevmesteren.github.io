This week's fintech radar is dominated by three converging structural shifts that change what competitive advantage means in banking.

**1. Canada's Real-Time Rail launches — without the Big Six.** Payments Canada's RTR went live November 9 after 11+ years of development. with eight launch members including Wealthsimple (largest Canadian participant), KOHO, and Questbank. No Big Six bank is in the launch cohort. The RTR commoditizes payment settlement — instant 24/7 money movement becomes infrastructure, not differentiation. Wealthsimple now controls three layers: the AI assistant (Penny), the yield-bearing deposit (CADD stablecoin), and the payment rail. BNC is absent.

**2. AI agents embed into B2B payments infrastructure.** Pliant raised $80M Series C explicitly positioning for agent-driven payment workflows. The signal: competitive advantage is shifting from product features to infrastructure control points — agent-ready payment APIs are the new platform layer.

**3. Stablecoin custody goes institutional.** Deutsche Bank launched digital asset custody after a five-year build. Wealthsimple became the first regulated Canadian FI to offer a CAD stablecoin (CADD). The tokenized assets market grew from ~$10B to ~$39B in 21 months.

**What to watch.** Blue J's $176M Series E (Toronto, AI tax research), Imprint's $130M (AI-powered co-branded cards), and $600M+ across 14 deals signal continued investor confidence in vertical AI despite a "slow deal" narrative. The RegTech/SupTech convergence is underway — the divide between compliance automation and supervisory technology is about to disappear.
