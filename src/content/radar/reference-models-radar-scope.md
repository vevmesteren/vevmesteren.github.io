Every Tuesday at 18:00 UTC, a scheduled job scans the open web for banking reference models, data standards, and interoperability frameworks. Not what banks say about standards. Who is actually defining the rails the ecosystem runs on.

The radar watches eight search areas: BIAN service landscape, FDX financial data exchange, ISO 20022 message definitions, Canada consumers-driven banking (CDB), global open banking standards (UK OBIE, EU PSD3, Australia CDR, Brazil, Singapore, Hong Kong), data models and semantics, interoperability and convergence, and adoption signals and certifications. Each finding gets mapped to reference model categories, assessed through six standards lenses, and ranked by signal strength: new, strengthening, or fading.

The lenses are the point. Six questions that turn standards signals into strategy:

- **Standards leverage**: which standards create competitive advantage by being first to adopt?
- **Interoperability position**: where does the bank sit on the interoperability curve — leader, follower, or laggard?
- **Data model risk**: which data model choices create lock-in or migration risk?
- **Convergence direction**: where are multiple standards converging, and what does the merged standard look like?
- **Jurisdictional play**: which standards create cross-border advantages or barriers?
- **Build vs adopt vs influence**: should the bank build, adopt, or influence the standard?

The radar runs because standards define the playing field. The scan catches the signals that tell you which standards to adopt early, which to track, and which to help shape.
