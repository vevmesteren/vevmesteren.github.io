Three of Canada's Big Six banks now block personal AI agents from online banking. Nine days later, one of them put an AI agent to work in its own contact centre.

In the last week of October, RBC, TD and BMO said they block third-party personal AI agents from their authenticated digital properties. CIBC says it does not support agents opening or accessing accounts. Scotiabank will "limit" them. National Bank says it is monitoring. OSFI Superintendent Peter Routledge left the call to each bank, provided existing guidelines are met.

On November 6, BMO introduced AI Agent Assist for credit card service. It pulls transaction data, policy and case guidance together for the employee handling the call. That is 500,000+ calls a year at 12 to 14 minutes each. BMO's targets: 20 to 30 percent less time on eligible inquiries, and about 40 percent of eligible refund requests processed automatically.

A month earlier, the 2026 Evident AI Index ranked BMO #2 of 50 banks for reported AI use-case outcomes, the top Canadian bank in that category.

Put those together and a posture shows. Keep outside agents away from customer accounts. Build agentic capability inside the walls. Be able to show the governance.

I don't read the block as permanent. TD said its block holds "while we ensure the appropriate safeguards, authorization mechanisms and oversight are in place." That is a bank buying time, and saying so.

The harder question is the one the block postpones. What does a bank do when the customer's main interface is an agent the bank doesn't control?

Blocking buys months. The answer still has to be built.
