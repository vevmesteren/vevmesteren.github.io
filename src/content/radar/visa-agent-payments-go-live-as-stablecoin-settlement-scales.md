Visa's Agentic Ready program arrived in Canada this week, and five of the Big Six — BMO, CIBC, RBC, Scotiabank, and TD — signed on as early issuing partners. The program puts banks in a controlled environment to test AI agent-initiated payments across enrollment, tokenization, authentication, and authorization. It is already live in the US, EU, LATAM, and APAC; Canada is the latest footprint. The payment authorization layer for agent-mediated commerce is now going live domestically. National Bank of Canada is notably absent from the early partner list.

Bain delivered the most architecturally precise North Star answer yet: the AI-native bank hinges on a deliberate split between deterministic systems — ledger integrity, payments, entitlements, regulatory controls — and probabilistic ones — agent orchestration, fraud intelligence, personalization. Where the boundary holds, it is defensible; where it blurs, regulatory exposure accumulates. Velocity beats efficiency: "faster trumps slimmer." Case studies back it — Bradesco on WhatsApp, Chime at seventy percent AI-served support with the top NPS in Q1, NatWest compressing campaign cycles from sixty days to one.

Stablecoin settlement crossed to production scale in the same window. SoFiUSD with Mastercard hit $25 billion annualized run rate, Visa settled $20 billion, Stripe went live in over a hundred countries, and Solana delivered DvP at four-hundred-millisecond finality under a cent. Then SAP Pay embedded stablecoin settlement into the ERP running eighty-four percent of global commerce, and Citi connected $6 trillion in daily volume via Coinbase. The stablecoin is becoming invisible middleware — and when the rail commoditizes, the trust infrastructure above it gets more valuable, not less.

National Bank of Canada, meanwhile, deployed Sardine's agentic AI risk platform — the first publicly announced agentic AI production deployment at a Canadian D-SIB — and OSFI finalized E-23, extending model risk management to AI, ML, and third-party vendor software.

Why it matters: agent payment rails, stablecoin settlement, and AI-native bank architecture all moved from framework to execution in the same week. The liability gap — who is responsible when an agent makes a payment that goes wrong — remains unfilled, and the bank that fills it becomes indispensable.
